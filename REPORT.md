# soft-CORN: is it a stand-alone ML contribution?

Benchmark report, second full run on a new machine (2026-10-10), the extension studies of FUTURE_WORK.md
(2026-10-11), the in-vehicle study with natively set-valued labels (2026-10-16) and its K-curve (2026-10-17). Hardware: RTX 5080, torch 2.13 / CUDA 13.0. All numbers come from `results/summary.md`,
`results/checks.md`, `results/extensions.md`, `results/setpred.md` and `results/*.csv`, which
`scripts/05_analyze.py`, `07_checks.py`, `09_setpred.py`, `10_extensions.py`, `14_provoice.py` and `16_kcurve.py` regenerate from the run logs.
Everything was re-run from the raw data. The frozen-feature benchmark now has **10 seeds**; the m ablation,
the checks, the replay-based analyses and the fine-tuning have 5.

## Verdict (short)

* **Correct, but not new as a method.** soft-CORN equals cross-entropy between the soft label
  and the CORN-decoded PMF. That is the fractional-count likelihood of a continuation-ratio
  (sequential-logit) model with level-specific effects. Reviewers at an ML venue will see the
  loss itself as known.
* **Empirically it is a strong, safe choice, not a clear winner.** Linear probes on 5 datasets ×
  up to 5 training sizes (22 problems, 10 seeds) give the picture below. Ties use a region of
  practical equivalence (ROPE) of 0.005 nats.
  * vs **soft-CORAL**, the published competitor: practically tied on likelihood (NLL −0.001; 5 wins, 17 ties,
    0 losses; P(practically equivalent) = 0.67). soft-CORN is better on set-MAE (−0.019; 8 wins, 0 losses;
    P(better) = 1.00), on gapped sets (linear heads only) and at larger training sizes. soft-CORAL is
    better at ≤ 512 training items (P(soft-CORN worse) = 0.78).
  * vs **soft cross-entropy**: indistinguishable overall (NLL: 2 wins / 16 ties / 4 losses;
    P(ROPE) = 0.89). soft CE is slightly ahead on the largest datasets.
  * Clearly better than every hard-label loss, the OLDL losses (Wen et al., ICCV 2023) and
    partial-label likelihood on NLL, and far better than SORD, CDW-CE and unimodal heads (section 5.2).
  * **With MLP heads** (10 problems, 10 seeds), the four proper soft losses are statistically tied on NLL, with
    soft-CORN ahead of soft-CORAL (−0.010; P(better) = 0.93; −0.018 on the full pools) and the gapped-set
    advantage gone.
  * **Varying the annotators per set** (m = 2, 3, 5) leaves these conclusions unchanged. With linear
    heads, soft-CORN's lead over soft-CORAL on gapped sets grows with m, from 0.031 to 0.051 nats.
  * **On larger K** (VADER sentiment, K = 9; ConvAbuse severity, K = 5) soft-CORN's lead over soft-CORAL grows
    (NLL −0.013 linear, −0.049 MLP; P(better) ≥ 0.98), while soft CE overtakes it on the full pools of the
    large VADER corpora.
* **The synthetic study explains the pattern, and it generalises.** Each output parameterisation wins on
  data generated by its own model. soft-CORN reaches about 10× lower excess risk than soft CE on
  continuation-ratio data, while soft-CORAL is about 10× better on proportional-odds data. On the
  two families that match no model, soft-CORN comes second both times. This persists across K = 3–10,
  d = 3–200, signal strength, label noise, rater heterogeneity and three set mechanisms other than m raters,
  and soft-CORN's lead over soft-CORAL grows with K (section 4.1).
* **What to train on.** Targets built from the ratings (their empirical distribution, or a 50/50 mix with
  the uniform-over-set label) are never significantly worse than the uniform-over-set target and are
  better in up to 13 of 30 synthetic problems; with a target-agnostic selection criterion and m = 5 they cut
  excess risk by about 30%. The exact set likelihood is no better than the empirical distribution. On real data
  all differences are inside the ROPE (≤ 0.003 nats). Uniform-over-set is a good default when only sets are
  available (section 5.1).
* **Fine-tuning: smaller and less clear than in the first run.** With the training-label-marginal head
  initialisation of the main protocol, soft-CORN beats soft-CORAL on 4 of 5 datasets (−0.051 nats at the
  main size, 10 seeds). **That margin depends on the head initialisation:** with LP-FT it shrinks to −0.009 over
  the 10 problems (≈ 0 at the main size), and when every
  method may use whichever initialisation, probe and learning rate validates best it is **−0.029 nats at the main
  size and −0.028 at 512 items** (CommitmentBank −0.073, Wiki Toxicity −0.041, MHS −0.028; SCUT and Humicroedit ≈ 0). A
  better-tuned LP-FT probe does not change this. Three more text backbones and image backbones (3 seeds) agree: the
  best-variant gap is −0.025 (DeBERTa-v3-base) and −0.020 (RoBERTa-large) on text and ≈ 0 (+0.002, +0.004) on SCUT with
  DINOv2 and SigLIP. soft-CORN and soft CE remain tied (section 5.5).
* **Calibration and set-valued predictions: the proper soft losses are tied.** Split-conformal prediction
  sets are valid for every method and equal in size for the proper soft losses, while hard-label losses need
  sets about 8% larger. Temperature scaling leaves every rank unchanged: proper soft losses are already
  calibrated (T ≈ 1), hard-label and partial-label losses are over-confident (T = 1.1–1.5).
* **Distribution shift and personalisation.** Holding out a platform, namespace, year or demographic subset
  costs all methods about the same, and soft-CORN beats soft-CORAL out of distribution (−0.010 NLL). With each
  rater's own ratings, per-rater offsets cut per-rating NLL by about 0.15 nats; when raters have few ratings the
  proportional-odds structure with per-rater thresholds is far more efficient.
* **Natively set-valued data (in-vehicle study): too few sets to decide, but a clear lesson on personalisation.** Only 98 of the
  1,446 answers (6.8%) are sets, so soft-CORN, PLL-CORN and CORN (hard) are tied on every metric except NLL against the
  uniform-over-set label. Across drivers no loss beats the label marginal. With a driver's own answers in training every loss does
  (NLL 1.54 to 0.82-1.14); soft-CORN has the lowest NLL of the twelve with MLP heads and is fifth with linear heads, where the
  softmax family leads, and the CORAL family is clearly worst (section 5.8).
* **Personalisation (K calibration answers of a held-out driver).** With a driver code and an MLP head, soft-CORN falls from NLL 1.60
  (K = 0) to 1.29 (K = 5) and 0.78 (K = 40); one or two answers buy 0.00 and 0.09. It beats soft-CORAL by 0.11-0.21 from K = 10 and ties
  soft CE and PLL-CORN. Its lead over hard CORN (0.07) is mostly the cost of the lower-median tie-break: training on a random member
  of each set is as good as training on the sets from K = 20 (section 5.9).
* **Recommendation.** A pure "new loss" paper would be hard to place at a main ML venue. Two
  framings could work: (i) a benchmark/analysis paper on learning from set-valued ordinal labels,
  with soft-CORN as the rank-consistent CORN counterpart of soft-CORAL (venues: TMLR or a
  datasets-and-benchmarks track); or (ii) keep soft-CORN inside the application paper, with this
  benchmark as supporting evidence. As a software contribution, add probabilistic-target support to
  `dlordinal`'s CORN loss (section 10).

## 1. Question

The thesis proposes **soft-CORN**: CORN (Shi et al., 2023) trained on a set-valued ordinal label S.
It replaces CORN's hard subset membership 1[y ≥ k] and indicator 1[y > k] by their expectations
under the uniform distribution u_S over the accepted set. Could this be split off as an ML paper?
That would need (a) a contribution reviewers accept as new and (b) evidence that it beats the
closest published alternative, **soft-CORAL / soft-OR** (Matton et al., 2026), and the standard
label-distribution baselines.

## 2. What soft-CORN is, formally (all checked numerically in `tests/`)

Let p be the soft label, q_k = P_p(y > k), and z_k the CORN conditional logits.

1. **It is a categorical likelihood.** Per example,
   L = −Σ_k [q_k log σ(z_k) + p_k log(1 − σ(z_k))] = −Σ_k p_k log p̂_k,
   where p̂ is the PMF that CORN's chain rule decodes. soft-CORN is therefore cross-entropy between
   the soft label and the continuation-ratio (sequential-logit) PMF. Equivalently, it is the
   grouped-data / fractional-count likelihood of the continuation-ratio model with level-specific
   effects (Fienberg, 1980; Tutz, 1991). `test_soft_corn_is_cross_entropy_of_decoded_pmf`.
2. **It reduces to CORN on one-hot labels.** This is exact with CORN's normalisation by the
   conditional-set size, and holds up to a constant per batch with per-example normalisation.
   `test_soft_corn_equals_corn_on_one_hot`.
3. **Gradient and curvature match the thesis appendix:** ∂L/∂z_k = q_{k−1} σ(z_k) − q_k and
   ∂²L/∂z_k² = q_{k−1} σ(1−σ). `test_soft_corn_gradient_and_hessian`.

Consequences for positioning:

* soft-CORN, soft-CORAL, soft-OR and soft cross-entropy are all **strictly proper for the same
  target**. In a rich enough model class, their population minimiser is t(x) = E[u_S | x]. They
  differ only in the output parameterisation:
  * continuation ratio with level-specific weights (CORN);
  * proportional odds with one shared weight vector (CORAL);
  * independent cumulative logits without rank consistency (OR);
  * multinomial logit (softmax).

  A linear soft-CORN head and a linear softmax head have the same effective number of parameters,
  (K−1)·(d+1), because softmax is invariant to a common shift of its logits.
* The three OLDL losses are **not** proper for t:
  * CAD (L1 between CDFs) is minimised by the coordinatewise *median* CDF of the labels.
  * QFD as published puts an absolute value inside the quadratic form.
  * CJS is a Jensen–Shannon term on cumulatives.

  Over 8 random three-label problems, the minimiser of the expected loss misses t by up to 0.14
  (QFD) and 0.17 (CJS) in total variation. For CAD, the median CDF beats the mean CDF by up to 0.21
  in expected loss. soft-CORN, soft-OR/CORAL and soft CE recover t exactly
  (`test_properness_for_the_mean_soft_label`).
* Neither the partial-label likelihood −log P̂(S) (whose target is the coarsened distribution of
  sets) nor a hard-label loss on the set's median targets t either.
* **The exact set likelihood is a different target.** When a set comes from m independent raters, the
  probability of observing exactly S under a predicted PMF π̂ is P(S | π̂) = Σ_{T⊆S} (−1)^{|S|−|T|} π̂(T)^m. Its
  maximiser is the rater distribution π itself (not E[u_S | x]); `exact_set_logp` implements it and the tests
  check it against enumeration of all K^m rating tuples.

So the *method* is a known model fitted with a known likelihood. An ML paper would have to rest on
an empirical finding, or on an application.

## 3. Benchmark design (details in README.md)

* **Real data.** Five public multi-annotator ordinal datasets with K = 4–7:
  * text: MHS sentiment, Wikipedia Toxicity, Humicroedit, CommitmentBank;
  * images: SCUT-FBP5500.

  Each set label is the set of levels chosen by 3 sampled annotators. The remaining annotators
  are held out and used only to evaluate (*held-out-rater NLL*). Features come from frozen
  encoders (all-mpnet-base-v2, OpenCLIP ViT-B/16). **Further datasets** (section 5.3): four VADER sentiment
  corpora (K = 9, ≥ 15 raters per item; 4,200 / 5,190 / 10,605 / 3,708 items) and ConvAbuse (K = 5, 4,185
  exchanges with 2–7 annotators, 3,091 with at least 3).
* **Synthetic data.** Five rater-distribution families with logits linear in x. Three are the home
  models of CORAL (proportional odds), CORN (continuation ratio) and softmax (multinomial logit);
  two match none of them: location-scale, and a polarised mixture that produces gapped sets. Labels
  again come from 3 raters. The exact population target gives the excess risk KL(t ‖ p̂) with no
  annotator noise. Variants change K, d, signal strength, label noise, rater heterogeneity, the set
  mechanism (m raters, an interval around a latent level, every level above a personal threshold, a mirror
  pair) and make the predictors nonlinear.
* **Methods.** The core twelve (soft-CORN; soft-CORAL and soft-OR; soft CE; OLDL-CAD/QFD/CJS; PLL-softmax and
  PLL-CORN; hard CE / CORN / CORAL) plus 20 in the extension studies: training targets built from the ratings
  (empirical, 50/50, exact likelihood) for the CORN, softmax and CORAL parameterisations; SORD (Laplace and
  Gaussian), CDW-CE, binomial and Poisson heads, and cumulative link models with logit, probit, cloglog and a
  learned (Aranda–Ordaz) link.
* **One protocol.**
  * Heads: linear at every training size (128, 512, 2,048, 8,192, full); a one-hidden-layer MLP
    as a robustness check.
  * Optimiser: Adam with an L2 penalty, 5 × 5 grid of learning rate and penalty, 2,000 steps.
  * Selection: the checkpoint and configuration for each test metric are chosen by the matching
    validation metric (set-MAE for set-MAE, NLL for NLL/held-out NLL/calibration/intervals, RPS
    for RPS). The target studies additionally select on the exact set likelihood `xnll`.
  * Shared across methods: data order, initialisation, splits, 10 seeds.
* **Metrics.** Set-MAE, NLL against the soft target, held-out-rater NLL, RPS, EMD, ordinal ECE against
  held-out raters, and prediction intervals: the narrowest contiguous set of levels with at least 50 / 80 /
  90 % of the predicted mass, scored by width, held-out-rater coverage, Jaccard distance to the observed set
  and a Winkler-type interval score.
* **Statistics.**
  * Per problem: Nadeau–Bengio corrected resampled t-test → win / tie / loss.
  * Across problems: Friedman + Nemenyi test, and the Bayesian signed-rank test with a ROPE of
    0.005 nats for NLL, 0.005 for set-MAE, 0.001 for RPS, 0.005 for ECE and 0.05 for the interval
    score. A random-effects meta-analysis across problems (DerSimonian–Laird; a stand-in for the Stan
    hierarchical test, which needs PyStan) and item-level paired bootstraps give a second view.

## 4. Results: synthetic (linear heads, 10 seeds; `figures/synthetic_klt_linear.png`)

| family (home model) | soft-CORN | soft-CORAL | soft-OR | soft CE | OLDL-QFD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| proportional odds (CORAL) | 0.0066 | **0.0008** | 0.0016 | 0.0061 | 0.0070 |
| continuation ratio (CORN) | **0.0013** | 0.2012 | 0.0577 | 0.0145 | 0.0178 |
| multinomial logit (softmax) | 0.0403 | 0.4371 | 0.1184 | **0.0016** | 0.0038 |
| location-scale (none) | 0.1072 | 0.1447 | **0.0973** | 0.1287 | 0.1327 |
| polarised mixture (none) | 0.0158 | 0.0465 | 0.0235 | **0.0124** | 0.0193 |

* **Each parameterisation wins on its home model**, with gaps of 10× or more that persist as n grows.
  So these are bias effects, not variance.
* **soft-CORAL fails off its home model.** Its shared score cannot follow item-level changes in
  spread. The correlation between predicted and true per-item spread is 0.34–0.49 on the
  non-proportional-odds families, against 0.75–1.00 for soft-CORN.
* **soft-CORN is second on both families that match no model.**
* **Hard-label losses, PLL and OLDL plateau well above the proper soft losses**, as their
  improperness predicts (section 2).
* **MLP heads (10 seeds, n ∈ {1,024, 16,384}).** The flexible parameterisations converge:
  * soft-CORN and soft CE are practically equivalent (P(ROPE) = 0.84).
  * soft-OR is slightly behind (P(soft-CORN better) = 0.72).
  * soft-CORAL stays far behind on every family except proportional odds (soft-CORN wins 8 of
    10 problems, loses 1): at n = 16,384 its excess risk is 0.19 on continuation-ratio data and 0.37 on
    multinomial-logit data, against ≤ 0.006 for soft-CORN.
  * On proportional-odds data all four land within 0.0024–0.0025.

  With enough capacity, the choice among soft-CORN, soft CE and soft-OR stops mattering, but
  CORAL's single-score restriction does not go away.
* **Calibration against the true rater distribution** (`ho_ece`, exact because the held-out
  distribution is the generating one; n = 16,384, linear heads): the home model is best calibrated
  on its own data, and soft-CORAL's ECE degrades off its home family (0.042 on multinomial-logit
  data, against 0.017 for soft CE and 0.022 for soft-CORN). Hard-label CORN has ECE 0.10–0.15 on
  every family.

* **soft-CORN's advantage over soft-CORAL holds everywhere it held before and grows with K** on
  continuation-ratio data (111 → 191 → 138 for K = 3, 8, 10). soft-CORAL keeps its proportional-odds
  home advantage except at K = 3, where the two parameterisations coincide almost exactly.
* **Label noise, rater heterogeneity and the three other set mechanisms shrink the gaps** (they add

* Against soft CE the ordering is the home-model one: in the plain variants soft-CORN is 2–20× better on continuation-ratio data
  and soft CE 5–50× better on multinomial-logit data, with the margins shrinking under label noise and the non-rater set
  mechanisms; for mirror and threshold sets soft CE wins four and three of five families.

* **Gapped sets:** soft-CORN beats soft-CORAL by 0.064 nats and 0.023 set-MAE, and soft CE by

* **Singleton sets:** soft CE is slightly better (0.018 nats, 0.004 set-MAE).

**Other observations:**

* **Selecting on set-MAE, as the thesis does, damages calibration, for every method.** Compared with
  NLL-based selection, the set-MAE-selected checkpoint raises test NLL (full pool, worst dataset;

  * 0.05 for soft-CORAL, 0.10 for soft-CORN and 0.11 for soft CE (means over datasets: 0.03, 0.03, 0.04);
  * 1.2 for soft-OR;
  * 0.65 (PLL-CORN) and 0.94 (PLL-softmax);
  * 6.5–11.0 for the OLDL losses.

  The first run measured 0.38 for soft-CORN; that was noise from a single run, and soft-CORN is hit no more
  than soft CE. The ordering (proper soft losses mildly, soft-OR, PLL and OLDL severely) is stable.
  Probabilistic claims in the thesis should still use NLL-based selection.
* **soft-OR is not rank-consistent.** Its implied P(y > k) is non-monotone for 1.7% of test items
  (NLL selection) up to 42% (set-MAE selection). CORAL and CORN never are.
* **All methods are close to the feature-free baseline on CommitmentBank and Humicroedit.** On
  CommitmentBank the best NLL is 1.840 against a prior of 1.923, and on Humicroedit 1.250 against
  1.272, so differences there are small in absolute terms.

### 5.1 What to train on: the target built from the ratings (`results/extensions.md`, `ext_targets_*.csv`)

Same sets, four training targets, for the CORN, softmax and CORAL parameterisations: the uniform-over-set label
(the benchmark's), the empirical distribution of the m ratings behind the set, a 50/50 mixture, and the exact set
likelihood. Targets differ, so NLL against uniform-over-set labels favours the uniform target; the comparison
therefore also selects checkpoints on `xnll`, the exact likelihood of the observed validation sets.

* **Synthetic (30 problems: 5 families × 2 sizes × m ∈ {2, 3, 5}), KL to the true rater distribution, other
  target minus uniform:** empirical −0.0025 (CORN; 7 wins, 0 losses), 50/50 mix −0.0022 (13 wins, 0 losses), exact
  likelihood −0.0006 (8 wins, 1 loss); the picture is the same for softmax and CORAL targets. The gain grows with m: at m = 5
  and n = 16,384 with `xnll` selection, soft-CORN's excess risk falls from 0.0549 (uniform) to 0.0387 (empirical)
  and 0.0394 (exact), about 30%; at m = 2 the targets coincide exactly. Selecting on NLL against uniform-over-set
  labels hides part of the gain (0.0561 → 0.0506 at m = 5).
* **The exact likelihood is not better than the empirical distribution** except where its home model applies
  (continuation-ratio data, CORN parameterisation: 0.0024 against 0.0046 for uniform and 0.0054 for empirical, m = 3,
  n = 16,384); it has higher variance. Its step costs 21% more (section 8).
* **Real data (32 problems: dataset sizes at m = 2, 3 and 5), held-out-rater NLL:** every
  difference is within ±0.003 nats (empirical −0.0015, 50/50 −0.0023, exact +0.0001 for CORN) and the Bayesian
  P(practically equivalent) is ≥ 0.97 for the empirical and mixed targets, so they are practically equivalent
  to the uniform target.
* **Reading.** The uniform-over-set label throws away the multiplicity of the ratings, which matters when
  sets are large (m = 5) and data plentiful, but the loss is small, and for natively set-valued labels (where the ratings behind a set
  do not exist) it is the only target available. This answers FUTURE_WORK item 4: the uniform target is not the weak
  point of soft-CORN.

### 5.5 Fine-tuned backbones (`figures/finetune_vs_frozen_nll.png`, `figures/finetune_nll_vs_size.png`)

all-mpnet-base-v2 for text, the OpenCLIP ViT-B/16 image tower for SCUT.
* Data: 2,048 training items (CommitmentBank: its 840) and 512 items, so each result sits next to a
  frozen-probe cell on the same items.
* Optimiser: AdamW, bf16, 5 epochs (8 for CommitmentBank).
* Learning rates: backbone {1e-5, 3e-5}, chosen per method on seed 0's validation NLL and reused;

### 5.7 Personalisation (`ext_personal.csv`, SCUT, 45 known and 15 held-out raters)

Predict a given rater's rating of a face from frozen features plus a per-rater offset of the output logits (the
rater-bias model, shrunk by the L2 penalty; held-out raters get a zero offset). The training target mixes the rater's
own rating with the population consensus (the uniform distribution over the set of 3 raters' ratings): α = 0 own
rating only, α = 1 the consensus. Per-rating NLL on held-out faces, known raters:

| ratings per rater | loss | α = 0 with offsets | α = 0 population model | α = 1 (offsets change nothing) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | soft-CORN | 1.064 | 1.071 | 1.053 |
| 50 | soft-CORAL | **0.972** | 1.062 | 1.051 |
| 50 | soft CE | 1.080 | 1.089 | 1.062 |
| 400 | soft-CORN | 0.905 | 1.046 | 1.047 |
| 400 | soft-CORAL | **0.888** | 1.050 | 1.048 |
| full (3,850) | soft-CORN | **0.881** | 1.038 | 1.046 |
| full (3,850) | soft-CORAL | 0.878 | 1.045 | 1.049 |

* **Per-rater offsets help a lot when the rater's own ratings are used** (−0.15 nats with enough data), and nothing
  when the target is the population consensus (identical numbers, a check on the implementation).
* **With little data per rater, the proportional-odds structure wins decisively**: 50 ratings per rater give 0.972
  for soft-CORAL (per-rater thresholds) against 1.064 for soft-CORN and 1.080 for soft CE; with 400 or more ratings the three
  are within 0.03 and soft-CORN is level with soft-CORAL at the full data (0.881 against 0.878). This is the one place
  where CORAL's restriction is clearly useful, and it matters for the thesis's personalised-proactivity setting, where
  each user contributes few labels.
* **Held-out raters are served worse by the personalised models** (zero offset: 1.19 for soft-CORAL at 50 ratings against 1.04
  for the population model), because the shared weights co-adapt to the offsets. A model for unseen users needs the
  population head or a prior over offsets.

### 5.8 Natively set-valued labels: the in-vehicle study (`results/provoice.md`, `provoice_*.csv`, `figures/provoice_pairwise*.png`)

Everything above builds sets from sampled raters. Here the sets are what people answered.

**Data.** ProVoice study 1 (CC-BY-NC-4.0, Hugging Face `ProVoice-proactivity/proactivity_preference_dataset`): 12 drivers in
a driving simulator, two sessions each. Every 20 s a driver was asked about two of five in-vehicle tasks and marked every
level of autonomy (LoA 0 = do nothing ... 4 = act autonomously) they would accept. There are 1,446 answers and **98 of them
(6.8%) are sets**: 97 contiguous (`1;2` x40, `0;1` x30, `2;3` x17, `3;4` x5, `0;1;2;3` x4, `1;2;3` x1) and one with a gap
(`0;3`). Answers per level, LoA 0 to 4: 512, 319, 225, 178, 319. Driver identity explains 21% of the entropy of the (lower-median)
answer, the task 13% (`ordbench/provoice.py` builds the table; the entropies come from the label file).

**Features.** 45 per answer: summaries of the ~20 Hz driver-state and vehicle frames inside the answer's 20 s window (means,
standard deviations and event fractions of gaze, blink rate, PERCLOS, heart rate, speed, steering, ...) and a one-hot code of the
task. The join reproduces the dataset card (723 windows, 254,141 labelled frames, 80-396 frames per answer; `tests/test_provoice.py`).
The thesis uses a 12-number task descriptor (FCD); with five tasks the one-hot code carries the same information.

**Protocol.** The driver is the group that is never split: 3 test drivers, 2 validation drivers, 7 training drivers, 20 random
draws (`03_run.py --m 0 --test_frac 0.25 --val_frac 0.17`; the defaults of every other dataset are unchanged), 512 answers or the
full pool (about 840), linear and MLP heads, the core twelve losses (the nine extra baselines on all features only), checkpoints
selected on validation as everywhere else. There are no held-out raters, so the held-out metrics and the rating-based targets do not

## 7. Robustness checks run

* **Grid edges, frozen linear heads.** 28.5% of selections took the strongest L2 penalty, more often
  for soft-CORAL (30%) than soft-CORN (16%). Re-running CommitmentBank and Humicroedit with the
  penalty grid extended to 1,000 (`results/wd_check`) changed mean test NLL by at most 0.0023 for
  every method (soft-CORN −0.0004, soft-CORAL −0.0008) and never selected the new maximum for
  soft-CORN or soft-CORAL (≤ 7.5% for any method). The soft-CORN minus soft-CORAL difference moved
  from +0.0028 to +0.0032 nats, so the ranking is unchanged.
* **MLP learning-rate edge.** The lowest learning rate (3e-4) was selected 40% of the time for
  soft CE, 28% for soft-CORN and never for soft-CORAL. I re-ran those three methods with learning
  rates {3e-5, 1e-4, 3e-4, 1e-3} and checkpoints every 50 steps (`results/mlp_lr_check`).
  * Choosing between both grids on validation NLL changed mean test NLL by −0.003 (soft CE) and
    −0.001 (soft-CORN); the largest single change was 0.04.
  * The new lowest rate was chosen 10% and 6% of the time and the first checkpoint 6% and 4%.
  * soft-CORAL selected the top rate (1e-3) in all 50 cells, i.e. it needs the higher rates
    that only the main grid covers.

  Every method's optimum therefore lies inside the main grid, and the MLP results stand.
* **Fine-tuning head initialisation.** In the first fine-tuning attempt, the output head trained at
  the backbone's learning rate from its default initialisation and soft-CORAL stayed worse than the
  feature-free prior (validation NLL about 2.55 on CommitmentBank). Initialising the biases at the
  training-label marginal fixed the failure, but section 5.5 shows that a head fitted on frozen
  features helps soft-CORAL further. CORAL-style heads are the most sensitive to this choice, and
  fine-tuning comparisons should state it.
* **Fine-tuning learning rate.** See section 5.5: a rate above the main grid changes little.
* **Hyperparameter sensitivity** (`results/sensitivity.csv`; share of the 25-point lr × L2 grid
  within 0.01 / 0.05 nats of a method's best validation NLL; linear heads, 10 seeds): soft CE 0.14 / 0.33,
  soft-CORN 0.13 / 0.29, soft-CORAL 0.12 / 0.30, soft-OR 0.09 / 0.18. soft-OR is the most
  sensitive (median excess validation NLL over the grid 0.74, against 0.14–0.21 for the others).
  With MLP heads soft-CORN is the least sensitive of the four proper soft losses (0.21 / 0.45) and
  soft-CORAL the most (0.13 / 0.37).
* **Optimiser.** Pilots showed that AdamW's decoupled weight decay does not regularise
  768–1,536-d linear heads: every method overfitted CommitmentBank to below the feature-free
  baseline. The benchmark therefore uses a coupled L2 penalty, the standard linear-probe protocol.
* **Implementation checks** (114 tests).
  * The vectorised ensemble trains each configuration exactly as an independent
    torch.optim.Adam run.
  * The CUDA-graph training path matches eager training.
  * The OLDL ports match the reference implementation.
  * soft-CORN matches `coral_pytorch.corn_loss` on one-hot labels.
  * The new interval and ECE metrics match brute-force loops; the exact set likelihood matches enumeration
    of all rating tuples; the PMFs of the unimodal heads match scipy; the generalised synthetic generator reproduces
    the original one and its set mechanisms match Monte-Carlo targets; the replay reproduces the logged metrics;
    the LP-FT standardisation fold is exact; the image encoder finds its feature size for open_clip towers with and without
    `output_dim` (SigLIP has none; the first SigLIP run crashed on it).
* **A failure that the first DeBERTa run exposed.** transformers 5 loads a checkpoint in its stored dtype and DeBERTa-v3-base is
  stored in fp16, where AdamW's epsilon underflows: every weight became NaN at the first step, and all 72 runs sat at the
  feature-free baseline (MHS NLL 1.415 against a prior of 1.417), identical for all methods. The tell was exactly that: a
  fine-tuned result no better than the label marginal. The encoders now always hold fp32 weights (with a regression test), the
  invalid runs are quarantined, and DeBERTa was re-run (section 5.5).
* **A bug that the tests caught.** The first "nonlinear" synthetic predictor (a random tanh network) was still 93%
  linear; it is now a quarter linear and three quarters pairwise interactions.

## 8. Compute cost (`results/cost.md`)

On the RTX 5080 (head on 768-d features, batch 256, K = 5; timed on an otherwise idle card after a warm-up pass), a
training step of the 25-configuration ensemble takes 7.7–9.2 ms for every core loss (soft-CORN 8.3 ms, soft CE 7.7 ms,
soft-CORAL 8.5 ms); the loss itself is 0.7–2.1 ms of a single-model step. The exact set likelihood costs 21% more
(10.0 ms) and this ratio is flat from K = 3 to K = 10 (1.18–1.28; peak memory grows from 67 to 218 MB with the 2^K − 1
subsets); a cumulative link model with a learned link is the slowest (14.4 ms, +75%). A first timing run, taken while
other jobs shared the card, gave the same ordering (7.7–9.1 ms). A linear soft-CORN head has (K−1)(d+1) = 3,076 parameters at
K = 5, d = 768, soft-CORAL 772, softmax 3,845. In fine-tuning the backbone dominates and the loss is negligible.

## 9. Limitations

* **Fine-tuning covers one text backbone and one image backbone in the main results, two training sizes (512 and
  2,048) and 5 seeds.** The full pools and 8,192 items have not been run, and per-seed learning-rate selection
  (`--lr_per_seed`) has not been used. The first LP-FT probe uses a small fixed grid.
* **Simulated set labels.** Sets come from sampled annotators (3 in the main runs, 2 and 5 in the
  ablation). The one native set-valued dataset (the in-vehicle study, section 5.8) has 6.8% sets, so it tests
  almost nothing about set-valued training; a dataset in which people mark several levels more often is still
  needed. The ablation varies set size but keeps the same mechanism.
* **The in-vehicle study uses window summaries, not the thesis's sequence model,** a one-hot task code instead of the 12-number
  descriptor, and 12 drivers (heavily overlapping splits, 2 validation drivers per split). Its known-driver setting gives a driver
  about 70 answers in training. The K-curve (section 5.9) follows the thesis's calibration protocol with one adaptation arm (a driver
  code with L2 shrinkage; no L2-SP or ANIL), random instead of assistant-chosen calibration answers, 3 seeds per driver, and test sets
  that shrink from about 120 answers (K = 0) to 54 (K = 40), validation sets from 242 to 110.
* **Held-out raters are thin on three datasets.** On MHS, most items have few annotators, so
  held-out-rater metrics rest on a subset of items; Humicroedit has only 2 held-out ratings per
  item; in ConvAbuse most items have none at m = 3.
* **Conformal sets calibrate on the data that selected the checkpoint** and are evaluated for linear heads only
  (replay needs independent members).
* **The shift study covers four datasets** with available group metadata; the personalisation study only SCUT
  (the only dense rater matrix) and only rater-bias offsets.
* **Per-dataset fine-tuning tests are weak** (5 seeds; 3 seeds for the DeBERTa, RoBERTa-large, DINOv2 and SigLIP backbones,
  whose rows carry no test); conclusions rest on the consistent direction across datasets, sizes and backbones.
* **No hierarchical Bayesian model with Stan** (PyStan does not install on this Windows / Python 3.13 setup); a
  random-effects meta-analysis and item-level bootstraps are used instead.

## 10. What a submission would need, and where the code should go

1. **A framing that does not claim the loss is new.** Cite the continuation-ratio grouped
   likelihood, and position soft-CORN as the CORN counterpart of soft-CORAL that drops the
   proportional-odds restriction.
2. **This benchmark, leading with two results:** the synthetic home-model result (which generalises across K,
   dimension, noise and set mechanisms, and grows with K), and the gapped-set advantage
   under linear heads, which grows with the number of annotators per set. The fine-tuning result is supporting
   evidence of moderate size (−0.02 to −0.03 nats against soft-CORAL on text backbones, ≈ 0 on image backbones, when every
   method gets its best head initialisation) and should be stated with that caveat.
3. **The properness analysis of the OLDL losses.** This is the most novel-looking finding here.
4. **A dataset in which native sets are common.** The in-vehicle study (section 5.8) is the first native set-valued data in the
   benchmark, but only 6.8% of its answers are sets, so it cannot carry a set-valued claim; the personalisation finding there
   (driver identity matters far more than the loss; soft-CORAL is worst once the driver is known; five calibration answers
   are worth far more than one or two) is the part to use, together with the finding that training on a random member of each set
   matches training on the sets (section 5.9).

**Where to contribute the loss.** [dlordinal](https://github.com/ayrna/dlordinal) (BSD-3, PyTorch, v2.7.0, active)
is the best home: its `CORNLoss` states that it [does not support probabilistic targets](https://dlordinal.readthedocs.io/en/stable/_modules/dlordinal/losses/corn_loss.html)
while the library already ships soft-labelling methods that produce such targets, so a probabilistic-target
`CORNLoss` (soft-CORN) is a visible gap with users beyond set-valued labels. Its README asks contributors to open an
issue first and to add tests and docstrings. [coral-pytorch](https://github.com/Raschka-research-group/coral-pytorch)
is the reference CORN implementation this repository tests against, but its [latest release is 1.4.0 from July 2022](https://pypi.org/project/coral-pytorch/)
and few issues are open, so a pull request there may sit unreviewed. Present it as a feature (probabilistic targets for CORN),
cite Fienberg and Tutz, and document where it helps (gapped sets, non-proportional-odds data) and where it does not
(ties soft cross-entropy, loses to soft-CORAL with little data).
